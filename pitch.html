You're the lead duck on a South African wine farm. The vines are crawling with snails. Clear them before the rain, without a drop of pesticide.

A bite-size pixel art arcade game: Pac-Man meets Untitled Goose Game, with a little Stardew Valley.

It's a true story

At Vergenoegd Löw, a wine estate near Stellenbosch in South Africa, about 1,000 Indian Runner ducks go to work every morning. They march out to the vineyards and eat the snails, aphids and worms off the vines, instead of the farm using pesticides.

The farm has done it for 40 years. The daily duck parade has become a tourist attraction, and neighbouring farms are starting to buy ducks of their own.

Source: RTÉ News, August 2026

How it plays

🦆

Lead the line

Steer your flock around the vineyard, Pac-Man style. Any duck that walks past a snail eats it.

🐌

Clear the block

Every block starts infested. Chain snails for combos, and clear a whole row for a satisfying "Row saved!"

🚶

Send patrols

Every duck behind you slows you down. Send some off on patrol, but they're gloriously dumb and stop at the first puddle.

🌧️

Beat the rain

Every shower brings fresh snails onto rows you've cleared. Can you finish before the next one?

Five days, then the harvest

Each day is a different vineyard block, with one upgrade each night: hatch a duckling, learn the Big Quack, sweep the puddles. At the end of the season your grapes become wine.

Where it is today

  • A playable prototype that works now: one HTML file, keyboard controlled, grey-box art. Play it here.
  • Three versions in, the loop is found. We tried a defend mode and a free-moving flock and parked both. The keeper is restore the block before the rain.
  • A backlog of ideas is ready: aiming patrols, a farm-dog "ghost", tourists on the duck parade, named ducks.
"Clearing rows feels great." (an early playtester)

What we'd build at the hackathon

Take the prototype and make it properly: something you'd show your friends.

  1. Real pixel art: waddling ducks, snails, vines, puddles, rain and a lovely harvest label
  2. Sound and music: quacks, a rising combo pitch, the "Row saved!" jingle and a cheerful farm tune
  3. Better patrols: aim when you split, and see where they'll go
  4. Hand-made maps in a level editor, so designers can build blocks without code
  5. A playable link plus a phone build, so the demo audience can play on their own phones

The demo: live play on the big screen, with everyone in the room competing for the best harvest.
